UNDER THE SCREEN

Physiology & evidence

A real equation, with explicit limits

Each parallel lung compartment obeys Paw + Pmus = PEEP + Vᵢ / Cᵢ + Rᵢ × Qᵢ. Inspiratory effort Pmus is positive assistance (a negative pleural-pressure swing). Q = dV/dt. Volumes are mL, time is seconds, C is mL/cmH₂O, and input resistance is converted from cmH₂O/(L/s) to cmH₂O/(mL/s).

Pressure-controlled steps use an exponential solution at 2 ms intervals: Vᵢ(t+Δt) = Cᵢ(Paw − PEEP + Pmus) + [Vᵢ(t) − Cᵢ(Paw − PEEP + Pmus)]e−Δt/(RᵢCᵢ). The two units share Paw but have different time constants, τᵢ = RᵢCᵢ. About 95% of equilibration takes 3τ and 99% takes 5τ. Residual elastic pressure produces intrinsic PEEP.

In VC, the model solves for airway pressure required for the target inspiratory flow; the pressure limit can truncate delivered flow. In PC, the pressure waveform rises to the pressure set above PEEP. Inspiratory pressure 16 with PEEP 6 gives an intended PIP of 22, not 16.

How modes behave here

  • A/C: patient efforts trigger a full time-cycled PC or VC breath. The set rate is a backup.
  • SIMV + PS: timed mandatory breaths can synchronize in a short window; intervening triggered breaths receive flow-cycled PS. PS is pressure above PEEP.
  • PRVC: PC breaths with breath-to-breath pressure adjustment toward target VT, limited to 2 cmH₂O per breath and bounded by minimum ΔP / maximum absolute Paw.
  • V+: flow-cycled, volume-targeted pressure support with timed apnea backup. This is not interchangeable with PRVC.
  • Auto: an illustrative PC-to-PS switch after three detected efforts, reverting to timed PC after the apnea interval. It is not ASV, AutoFlow or a reproduction of a proprietary Automode algorithm.
  • AVAPS: volume-assured PS with timed backup; pressure changes at a modeled maximum 2 cmH₂O/min. AVAPS is usually associated with noninvasive/chronic support; this no-leak model is not neonatal device guidance.
  • CPAP + PS: triggered support and PEEP, with a modeled timed apnea backup. PS = 0 demonstrates unsupported spontaneous breathing on CPAP.

Triggering occurs when simulated effort exceeds the pressure-trigger threshold plus residual elastic pressure. PS cycles when flow falls below the chosen fraction of peak flow, or at the set maximum inspiratory time. Real devices may use flow triggers, different synchronization windows, adaptive cycling and other backup logic.

Readouts and experiments

VTe is integrated exhaled volume over the preceding cycle; VTi integrates inspiratory flow. Total rate and minute ventilation use recent completed breaths. Volume waveforms show change relative to the start of each breath. Faded loops show the last three completed cycles; save a reference loop before changing settings.

Pplat est. is PEEP + total end-inspiratory elastic volume / total C. ΔP est. subtracts set and intrinsic PEEP. These are passive, equilibrated model estimates, not measured dynamic plateau pressures. The inspiratory hold occludes net airway flow for 0.4 s while compartments equilibrate; patient effort can distort measured Paw. No expiratory hold is simulated. Loop curves retain every physics step (at most 2 ms apart), independently of the lighter time-waveform history. Pressure-targeted breaths have a smooth inspiratory rise and a modeled 12 ms expiratory pressure-decay time constant; these are simplified controller assumptions, not a manufacturer valve model. VC retains ideal constant-flow switching. Straight sections can therefore be expected in some modes; no cosmetic curve fitting is applied.

Compliance has a smooth heuristic PEEP recruitment term and an overdistension penalty. Shunt falls modestly with PEEP and surfactant. These curves are assumptions, not fitted neonatal pressure-volume data. Treatment toggles apply fixed effects: surfactant C ×1.8 and shunt −6 percentage points; bronchodilator R ×0.55; suction R ×0.75; sedation effort ×0.3 and rate ×0.6; paralysis removes effort; caffeine restores a minimum rate of 40 and effort of 3. These are teaching perturbations, not efficacy estimates or treatment recommendations.

Gas exchange is a teaching approximation

Get a gas takes a snapshot of the gas model. Displayed results stay unchanged until the next draw; the sample time and age remain visible. The model continues evolving between draws. The challenge mode requires a sample after the latest setting or intervention change and no older than 60 simulated seconds. Challenge goals are model-specific teaching objectives, not clinical diagnostic criteria. A correct working diagnosis, the relevant intervention or adjustment, the last three breath volumes, gas goals and pressure limits must stay satisfied for 10 simulated seconds. Reveal answer ends an attempt without marking it completed.

Alveolar minute ventilation V̇A = rate × max(VTe − dead space, 0). PaCO₂ tends toward 0.863 × V̇CO₂ / V̇A (V̇CO₂ in mL/min and V̇A in L/min), bounded at 12–180 mmHg to keep the model numerical. At zero ventilation, oxygenation is not physiologically modeled; the screen labels gases unreliable.

Alveolar PO₂ ≈ FiO₂ × (760 − 47) − PaCO₂/0.8. Arterial oxygen content mixes ventilated end-capillary content and a fixed mixed-venous content (65% saturation) according to shunt. Content = 1.34 × Hb × saturation + 0.0031 × PO₂. A Hill curve (exponent 2.7, editable P50) converts content back to arterial PO₂ and saturation. Fetal Hb, pH, temperature, cardiac output, regional V/Q and changing venous oxygen extraction are not dynamically modeled. Saturation is an arterial estimate, not a pulse-oximeter simulation. HCO₃⁻ is fixed in the Henderson–Hasselbalch pH estimate.

Clinical evidence

  1. CAHS Neonatology · Conventional ventilation

    Clinical reference for breath triggering, cycling, time constants, waveforms and neonatal settings.

  2. Sweet et al. · European RDS consensus, 2022 update

    Supports volume-targeted ventilation, an initial VT around 5 mL/kg, and the roles of surfactant and caffeine. It does not validate the effect sizes in this model.

  3. European RDS consensus · 2025 update

    Updated neonatal RDS guidance; reinforces lung-protective and volume-targeted approaches. Check the original for recommendations and context.

  4. Emeriaud et al. · PALICC-2, 2023

    PARDS: suggests VT 6–8 mL/kg (lower if needed), plateau ≤28 cmH₂O in usual chest-wall mechanics, and static driving pressure ≤15 cmH₂O. Evidence certainty varies. Scenario targets are educational and not universal clinical limits.

  5. Saddi et al. · Pediatric AVAPS, 2022

    Review of pediatric volume-assured support, device limits and limited evidence. AVAPS implementations and minimum deliverable volumes vary.

  6. Philips · AVAPS device instructions

    Manufacturer description of gradual pressure support adaptation to a volume target; not validation of this simulator.

Evidence reviewed November 25, 2026. This simulator is unvalidated and cannot reproduce all neonatal or pediatric physiology. Case goals and alarms are teaching prompts, not bedside protocols.
